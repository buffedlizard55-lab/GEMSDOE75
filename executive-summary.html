Why the “values in [0, 1]” error happens

A raw feature raster, fault-label nodata such as −1, infinity, an unmasked sentinel, or an unnormalized model output can violate the prediction range. We cannot diagnose your earlier uploaded file because its bytes were not supplied. Do not simply rename a feature or label TIFF. A NaN is not a valid probability inside the footprint.

Our conservative export policy requires finite probabilities in [0,1] everywhere, with zero outside the footprint, no positive pixels on visible mapped faults, a single float32 band, and exact reference-grid alignment. This policy is stricter than some historical exports and is not proof of organizer acceptance. The shared writer rejects bad values rather than silently clipping them.

When a new candidate is approved

  1. Check the readiness receipt. It must show a candidate hash, successful format and full-scope novelty checks, feature canaries, comparable pooled HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator/count/CI, and separate selector approval.
  2. Download the named prediction. Use its unique .tif filename, or a ZIP containing exactly that one TIFF. Do not upload the JSON receipt, report, training data, or sample.
  3. Verify file integrity. Compare SHA-256 with the published receipt. Confirm exact CRS, dimensions and geotransform against the organizer template; do not rely only on the filename.
  4. Check the authenticated contest page. Read current eligibility, data rules, deadline and remaining weekly cap. None is freshly verified here.
  5. Use New submission. Choose the TIFF/ZIP in “File to submit.” Paste the published note (at most 140 characters). This site does not upload or consume slots for you.
  6. Preserve the receipt. Record the organizer's submission identifier, score, timestamp and file hash. Only then label the result ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED. Rejection is not a successful submission.
